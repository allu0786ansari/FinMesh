# Proposal amendments

Changes to `FinMesh_Proposal_v2.md` after it was finalised. Each is made before the results it affects exist, records who decided it, and is marked in the proposal text.

| ID | Date | Section | Change | Reason | Decided by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | 24 Oct 2026 | 7.1, 9 | Model and evaluate on the dense period only (1–10 Sep 2022). The window is found by a label-free rule: longest run of days with at least 5% of the median daily row count. | Week-1 finding 1: the sparse tail (11–18 Sep) is about 60% laundering because licit traffic has stopped; including it would inflate precision. | Owner, on week-1 findings |
| A2 | 24 Oct 2026 | 7.2 | Rank banks by number of accounts, not transaction volume. | Week-1 finding 4: bank 70 has 15 accounts and 9% of traffic. | Owner, on week-1 findings |
| A3 | 24 Oct 2026 | 7.1 | "Most laundering transactions carry no pattern label" corrected to 38% unlabelled (HI-Small) and 71% (LI-Small). | Week-1 finding 5; factual correction. | Correction |
| A4 | 24 Oct 2026 | 7.2, 7.3 | Federation membership compared across real-bank (top *k*) and grouped-institution options on train and validation schemes only, with a selection rule fixed in code before real numbers exist; test schemes counted only for the chosen option. | Week-1 finding 3: four to eight real banks hold under 4% of accounts. | Rule proposed in week 2; **final choice at Gate G0** |
| A5 | 25 Oct 2026 | 7.2, 7.3 | Primary federation fixed by design: B8 (eight grouped institutions, every account covered); real-bank options become a secondary partial-participation analysis; projections use the validation rate only. Replaces the A4 selection rule. | The A4 rule projected from train + val, which overstates sparse federations (train schemes are rarely cut by a partition end). On HI-Medium it chose A64 (projected 135, actual 87), where two thirds of scheme legs are invisible to every member. The A64 test count was observed before this amendment; disclosed in `docs/gates/G0.md`. | Gate G0 |
| A6 | 25 Oct 2026 | 7.1, 13 | Headline dataset HI-Medium; LI-Medium second regime; HI-Small and LI-Small for development and replication. | G0 bands: HI-Small 73 and LI-Small 18 multi-member test schemes, below 100; the proposal's fallback is HI-Medium, which fits in memory (G0 peak 739 MB). | Gate G0 |
| A7 | 25 Oct 2026 | 4.2 (H3b), 11.1 | Grounding rate measured on JSON-only decoding; schema-constrained decoding kept as the guardrail and its failure rate reported separately. Primary model llama3.2:3b; language-model latency target p95 ≤ 30 s; narratives evaluated on a stratified sample of 200 incidents. | Both probed models scored 100% grounding under enum-constrained decoding, true by construction. | Gate G0 |
| A8 | 25 Oct 2026 | 8.1, 9 | Feature schema v2 replaces v1 before any model is trained: history and new-counterparty features use only the previous 72 h (`age_windows` becomes `recency_windows`); the first 72 h of the observation window are a training burn-in. Every feature now depends only on the current window and a fixed span before it. | v1 history features counted from the observation start, so they grew with time by construction (drift PSI 7 from train to validation on the fixture); a model trained on early windows would meet systematically different values later. Found by the week-4 drift diagnostic. | Owner, on week-4 review (no model results existed) |
| A9 | 25 Oct 2026 | 9 (Labels) | An account-window that touches laundering only incidentally is **masked** (neither positive nor negative): at least 20 legs in the window, of which under 10% are unmasked laundering legs. Alerts on masked windows still use budget. The original "contains a laundering transaction" label is reported as a sensitivity analysis. Scheme detection counts only non-incidental scheme account-windows. | Week-4 diagnostics: such windows are 2–6% of positives but all of the positives in the top 100 and top 1,000 by activity on HI-Medium and LI-Medium (busy accounts a scheme passed through once). Degree-based scorers would collect them as hits an analyst would not count. | Owner, at G1 preparation |
| A10 | 25 Oct 2026 | 11.1 | Primary operating point: 1 alert per 1,000 accounts per day per member (top-k per window). The 0.1% and 0.01% FPR budgets are secondary and always reported with their realised false-alert volume. | Week-3 finding: FPR thresholds fitted on one period overshoot on the next (up to 2x), so scorers would be compared at different effective budgets; the per-day rule fixes alert volume exactly and matches how analyst capacity is sized. | Owner, at G1 preparation |
| A11 | 25 Oct 2026 | 8.2 | Feature schema v3: neighbourhood lookback 72 h (from 24 h), the same span as the history features; otherwise identical to v2. | Week-4 diagnostics: median scheme durations are 22–52 h and only 31–51% of schemes fit in 24 h; cycle signatures fired on 19% of cycle schemes and scatter-gather on 2%, which would confound the visibility gap with the lookback. | Owner, at G1 preparation |
| A12 | 26 Oct 2026 | 11.2 (X1, X4) | Two analyses added at G1, before any sharing level exists: a **secondary artefact ablation** (S0 and the oracle without the payment-format shares and `self_tx`) and an **informational decomposition** (S0 alerts linked with the pooled rule). Provisional thresholds H1b, H1d and H2a unchanged. | G1 evidence: payment format and self-transfers are the strongest features in every model, likely AMLworld simulator quirks; the oracle's gain over S0 is almost entirely cross-member linking (any-alert detection differs by 2 points, linked detection by 51). | Owner, at Gate G1 |
| A13 | 26 Oct 2026 | 8.3, 11.2 (X1) | Fusion layer fixed before any real-data X1 result. **Candidates:** every validation account-window with probability ≥ 0.01 or an S0 alert asks about its cross-member counterparties (week 6 asked for alerts only). **Primary fusion:** a per-member logistic stacker (standardised, L2, C = 1) on the level's evidence features (own logit; requests, refusals and alert flags received; inbound requests and their buckets; at S2+ the counterparty record buckets; at S4 the mean counterparty embedding), cross-fitted on the two time halves of validation (windows 39–44 and 45–50), so no row is scored by a model fitted on it. Each member cuts its new score at the primary budget. **Linking:** final alerts at S1+ and propagation use the pooled rule (week 6 showed the S1 protocol recovers it, minus 130 rate-limited requests on HI-Medium); S0 keeps the local rule. **Secondary rules**, reported, not selected: noisy-OR (flag weight = the member's alert precision on the other half; record weight = its probability-bucket midpoint) and k-of-n (k = 1). **Propagation** (plaintext, no cryptography): own probability + 0.5 × strongest cross-member counterparty probability in the lookback. **Seeds** 0–2 on validation; the 5 headline seeds apply at test. | Week 6: S1 and S2 alerts link identically (0.604) because both stand on S0's alerts, so a level can only add value by changing *which* accounts are alerted; that is triage, and its rule must be fixed before X1 numbers exist. The stacker is the least tuned rule that can use every level's evidence; the fixed rules show how much depends on fitting. | Researcher, week 7; owner to confirm at review |
