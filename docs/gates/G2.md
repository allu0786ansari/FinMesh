## Why H1c fails on validation

1. **S1's alert flags leak fan-in on LI-Medium.**
   - The excess over the prior is 0.058 [0.046, 0.071] at 1% FPR: alerted accounts tend to have high fan-in, and the flag says "alerted".
   - S3 carries the flag in the clear by design, so it inherits this (0.067–0.075). The ε-noised record adds about 0.015 on top.
   - This is a property of any alert-sharing scheme, not a defect of the implementation.
2. **On HI-Medium only the rarest target fails, and only on its upper bound.**
   - The target is pass-through accounts, 0.3% prevalence, about 270 positives in the test half.
   - At S3 with ε = 1 and 2, the point estimates are 0.022 and 0.015, but the upper bounds are 0.054 and 0.051.
   - Every other target is within the margin at every S3 ε, and S1 is within on all four.

**Recommendation: no further amendment.** Four amendments (A13–A16) were already prompted by validation. A minimum-positives rule for "assessable" targets, added now, would be shaped by this result. Test reports H1c as preregistered, with the power limitation of rare targets stated alongside.

## Other findings to carry into the paper

- **The fix worked.** After A16, S3's leakage matches S1's (LI fan-in AUC 0.72 for both; 0.89 before the fix), and A4 at S3 is at chance at every ε. Before A16, S3 leaked through whether a record existed, and the same noisy record could link an account across epochs.
- **Records add little utility and most of the leakage.**
  - S1 gets 0.94–0.98 of S2's gain.
  - S2 and S4 reveal the fan-in decile at TPR 0.88–0.93, and link an account across a key rotation half the time.
  - The frontier's efficient points are S1, S3 and PD at high τ.
- **S3 below S1 on LI-Medium.** With the existence leak removed, S3's 20 noisy record features add nothing the per-member stacker can use, and with LI-Medium's few positives they cost about 0.02–0.04.
  - The proposal's mitigation ("coarser buckets") was not applied; doing so now would be tuning on validation. Reported as a finding.
- **A5:** S4's disclosure does not reveal the autoencoders' training rows (AUC 0.50 on both datasets).
- **A6:** being asked raises a responder's TPR on its own laundering accounts by 0.075 (HI) and 0.024 (LI) at 1% FPR. Measured, not prevented.
- **PT2 depends on budget sizing.**
  - At the 250,000 placeholder the OPRF was only 2× safer than hashing.
  - Sized to legitimate use, it is 11× (HI) and 7.8× (LI).
  - The OPRF's protection is metering, logging and epoch rotation, and it is only as good as the budget.

## Frozen for test (week 18)

- **Levels:** S0, S1, S2, S3 (ε ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8}, A15–A16), S4, PD (τ ∈ {2, 3, 4}).
- **Pooled references:** the preregistered oracle, the relational oracle (A14, H1b's ceiling), oracle + propagation, plaintext propagation.
- **Fusion:** the A13 stacker, refitted on all of validation and frozen; noisy-OR and k-of-n as secondary rules.
- **Protocol parameters:**
  - token budgets per A16;
  - 7-day epochs;
  - 20 answers per token;
  - 72 h retention and lookback;
  - evidence floor 0.01.
- **Attacks A1–A6** as specified in A15–A16, with H1c margin 0.05.
- **Seeds:** S0, the oracle, S2 and PD use 5 seeds (0–4) at test; everything else uses 3.
