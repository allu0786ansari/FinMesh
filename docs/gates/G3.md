## Question this gate answers

Is the federated DP detector good enough, at which privacy unit, to join the deployed detector, and is its privacy accounting correct?

## Findings to carry into the paper

1. **DP tuning on public proxy data works and matters.** The same privacy budget gives 1.6× (HI) and 2.5× (LI) the AUPRC after one retune on separate data, with no Medium data used. The A18 grid failed because its learning rate was fixed at 1e-3.
2. **Cross-bank value comes from evidence sharing, not from a jointly trained model.** With graph features, each bank's own GBDT (HI 0.337) beats every federated neural model (best 0.310, centralised and non-private). Even the non-private federated model adds about half a point when stacked. RQ1 showed that sharing alert flags lifts linked detection from 0.17 to 0.73. Together they support one message: **collaborate on cases, not on models.**
3. **No measurable account-level membership leakage, even without DP.** A model trained on about 250,000 accounts per run for 1,000 steps does not memorise individual accounts in a way a loss-based attack can see. DP's value here is the formal guarantee, not an empirical defence. Stronger attacks (LiRA with shadow models; Carlini et al. 2022) are possible but outside the plan.
4. **The degree cap is the most useful privacy result of the DP track.** It cuts one account's reach through neighbours' features by more than 300×, for 0 to 0.02 AUPRC.
5. **H2a and H2b split by regime.** Both are reported as pre-registered; test decides.

## Caveats

- **The A19 winner sits on the grid's edge (clip, lr).** A wider grid might do better; A19 allowed one retune only, so this is stated, not chased.
- **On HI-Medium the per-member stacker is slightly below the raw GBDT** (−0.007). The ensemble's recalibration does not help there. The frozen RQ1 configurations use the local GBDT fixed at G2 and are unaffected.
- **The IID comparison is weak** because B8 members are similar by design. A real-bank federation (partial participation, A64) would test heterogeneity properly.
